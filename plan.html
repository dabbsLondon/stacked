// the idea

Cherry-pick climbs.
Stitch them into one ride.

Indoor cycling platforms like Rouvy accept GPX files as routes. The climbs you actually want to ride are scattered across maps — one in Yorkshire, one in Wales, one in the Lakes. Stacked lets you collect those climbs, splice them into one synthetic route, preview it on a map and elevation profile, and export a GPX that Rouvy will treat as a single ride. It also works in reverse — import a long ride, drag-cut the climbs you want out of it, save each as a standalone file. No backend. All in your browser.

// how it works

Three operations.
Done in your browser.

The whole engine is three transformations applied in order: shift coordinates to align endpoints, shift elevations to match altitudes, then optionally insert recovery segments between climbs.

01 — STITCH

Translate to align endpoints

Each climb's coordinates are translated (shifted on the lat/lon plane — no rotation, no scaling, no warping) so hill B starts exactly where hill A ended. Distance, gradient, and the shape of every metre of road are preserved perfectly.

The result is geographically synthetic — your stitched route on a real-world map looks like a squiggle wherever you anchor it — but mathematically continuous for Rouvy's distance and gradient maths.

02 — NORMALISE

Shift elevations to meet

If hill A ends at 200m and hill B's GPX starts at 400m, hill B's entire elevation profile is translated down by 200m. Every relative rise, every gradient percentage, every metre of total ascent is preserved — only the absolute altitude baseline shifts.

Your trainer cares about gradient, not altitude. Translating elevations is lossless for training, costs nothing, and decouples altitude continuity from your workout design.

03 — BRIDGE

Optional recovery segments

Once climbs are stitched and normalised, the junctions are seamless. Then you decide: should there be space between hill A and hill B? Drop in a bridge segment — flat road, a descent into a valley before the next climb, or a false-flat ramp. Configure by gradient (length derives) or by length (gradient derives).

Bridges are drawn in teal on the map and chart, so you can always tell engineered bridge from real GPX data.

// the other half

Cut climbs out of long rides.

Half your GPX files come from real rides — a 60km Saturday loop that happens to contain two climbs worth keeping. Stacked works in reverse too: import a long ride, drag handles to mark slices, save each as a standalone GPX. Those extracted files become ingredients you feed back into the stitcher.

CUT MODE

Slice → save → re-use

Drop a long GPX in. The full elevation profile renders end-to-end. Drag a selection across the chart — or pick start/end markers on the map — to highlight any segment. Add more selections from the same file. Each can be named and previewed independently.

Save and each slice exports as its own clean GPX with original metadata preserved. The output is identical in shape to anything you'd download from Climbfinder — ready to drag straight back into the stitcher on the other tab.

// where to get climbs

GPX files are everywhere.
You just need to know where to look.

For Stacked to be useful you need a steady supply of climb GPX files. Here are the best sources, ranked roughly by friction and quality. Most are free or have a free tier that's plenty for personal use.

Climbfinder
free
climbfinder.com
Catalogue of named climbs across Europe with GPX downloads. Best first stop for known European cols — entries already isolate just the climb, no chaff.
cyclingcols
free
cyclingcols.com
Deep database of European cols with elevation profiles and GPX downloads. Especially strong on Alpine and Pyrenean climbs.
Strava
freemium
strava.com
Any public activity or route exports as GPX. Segments need a Premium subscription to download cleanly. The richest source if you have a sub.
Ride With GPS
freemium
ridewithgps.com
Huge library of user-uploaded routes. GPX export on the free tier. Good for British and US climbs that don't appear on Climbfinder.
Komoot
freemium
komoot.com
Route planner with strong UK and EU coverage. GPX export needs a one-time region purchase or premium account.
PJAMM Cycling
freemium
pjammcycling.com
Hyper-detailed climb database — gradient broken down per kilometre, GPX exports for paid members. Strong on iconic and obscure climbs worldwide.
Garmin Connect
free
connect.garmin.com
Your own activities and routes export cleanly to GPX. The best source if a climb you want to stitch is one you've actually ridden.
OpenStreetMap + SRTM
free
openstreetmap.org · opentopodata.org
Build your own GPX from any road on OSM. Enrich with elevation from SRTM via the free OpenTopoData API. Useful for fixing files with broken or missing elevation.

Sketch of the planned UI. Left rail is the order of climbs (drag to reorder, click to edit per-file settings, junction strip between each pair for the bridge config). Right is a live preview — map on top, elevation chart below.

left rail

Ordered list of climbs. Drag to reorder. Each climb shows distance, elevation gain, and average gradient. Click to expand for trim handles.

junctions

Between every pair of climbs, a bridge segment editor. Click the gear to set type, gradient, and length. Default: no bridge.

map

Full stitched route on Leaflet. Real GPX data in yellow, engineered bridges in teal. "SYNTHETIC ROUTE" badge so you don't confuse it with reality.

elevation

Complete profile end-to-end. Same colour code: yellow for real, teal for bridge. Hover for distance/gradient at any point.
